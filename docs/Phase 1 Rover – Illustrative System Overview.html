8 · Proposed pin map — main ESP32 PROPOSED—VERIFY

Assumes a 30/38-pin ESP32-WROOM-32 dev board. Module-side pins follow common breakout conventions (BTS7960/IBT-2, GY-521, GY-271, NEO-6M, DHT22) and must be checked against your exact boards. GPIOs were chosen to avoid strapping pins (0, 2, 5, 12, 15), boot-time PWM glitches (GPIO14) and flash pins, and to keep encoders on input-only pins.

9 · PCB-style wiring view — low-voltage signal board only PROPOSED—VERIFY

How to read this view

  • Dots are connector pins; each coloured line is one signal trace. Labels show ESP32 GPIO number and module pin function. Traces never cross.
  • Left side = sensor inputs; right side = outputs to drivers and links to other modules.
  • Symbolic only: this is a routing concept, not a footprint or Gerber layout. Real pin positions come from your dev board.

Trace colour key

Motor-driver logic   I²C   DHT22   CAM UART   Encoders   GPS UART   Battery sense

What each connector does

  • ① ② Driver logic: RPWM sets forward speed, LPWM reverse speed, EN enables the bridge. Add an external pull-down on EN (value TBD) so motors stay off at reset. Never drive RPWM and LPWM together on one side. These carry logic signals only; motor current goes on the separate harness.
  • ③ I²C: one shared two-wire bus to the MPU-6050 and QMC5883L. Check each breakout's address and how many pull-up resistors are already fitted before adding any.
  • ④ DHT22: single data line. It needs a pull-up per its datasheet unless the breakout includes one.
  • ⑤ CAM UART: TX goes to the camera's RX and RX to its TX (crossed), 3.3 V logic, shared ground. Only capture requests and status travel here, never drive commands.
  • ⑥ Battery sense: the midpoint of a resistor divider (R1/R2 TBD) feeds an ADC1 pin. Compute it so full-battery voltage stays inside the ADC limit.
  • ⑦ Encoders: four input-only pins. Confirm encoder type and output voltage first.
  • ⑧ GPS: GPS TXD goes to ESP32 RX (GPIO4). GPIO33 back to GPS RXD is optional.

10 · Architecture decisions & documentation notes

  • ADR-1 Single motor authority: only the main ESP32 drives PWM/EN; the CAM sees UART only. Consequence: camera faults cannot move the rover.
  • ADR-2 Enable pulled down in hardware: EN lines have external pull-downs (TBD value) so drivers stay off during reset/boot, independent of firmware.
  • ADR-3 Physical cut-off outside the logic board: acts on motor power, not on a GPIO. Implementation TBD.
  • ADR-4 Shared I²C bus: IMU and magnetometer on GPIO21/22; confirm addresses and pull-up count.
  • Datasheet honesty: I can't read your actual boards. Module pin names above are typical conventions; ESP32 GPIO choices are my proposal from known ESP32 pin constraints. Before building, confirm each against your datasheets, then change status tags to CONFIRMED.
  • Critique: fixes needed before layout: confirm logic voltage of every module, decide pull-down/divider values from datasheets, measure the 500 ms stop on hardware.

6 · Intended behaviors NOT TESTED RESULTS

  • Motors inactive at boot/reset; rover starts DISARMED. CONFIRMED IN DESIGN
  • Manual commands bounded (limits TBD). Stop on BLE disconnect or expired command.
  • Command-expiry stop timing: 500 ms — target; must be measured on the real rover.
  • Camera only captures when the operator asks; it cannot command motors.
  • Physical cut-off removes motor power regardless of phone, BLE or firmware state; circuit implementation TBD—DO NOT GUESS pending real driver and power hardware.
  • Out of scope: second main ESP32, Wi-Fi, cloud, MQTT, autonomy, waypoints, patrol, obstacle avoidance, AI security.

7 · Design critique notes

  • Hierarchy: safety banner first, then legend, chassis, power, logic. Red is reserved for stop/safety.
  • Accuracy: every unspecified link is dashed; no pins, values, models or dimensions invented.
  • Accessibility: tags rely on text and border style, not color alone; dark mode supported.
  • Known limit: the chassis view is topological, so the harness routing is symbolic.
